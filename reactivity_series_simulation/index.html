📖 Laboratory Manual: Reactivity Series

1. Single Displacement Reaction: A chemical reaction in which a more electropositive (reactive) metal displaces a less reactive metal from its aqueous salt solution:

A + BC → AC + B (where A is higher than B in the reactivity series)


2. Core Observations:

  • Iron in CuSO₄: Blue solution turns pale light-green ($\text{Fe}^{2+}$ ions form), while reddish-brown metallic copper coats the nail.
  • Zinc in CuSO₄: Solution rapidly decolors completely (blue to clear), dark spongy copper precipitates, with marked exothermic heat release.
  • Copper in AgNO₃: Solution gradually turns delicate azure blue ($\text{Cu}^{2+}$ ions dissolve) while sparkling silver needle crystals grow like a miniature frosty pine tree.
  • Copper in FeSO₄ / ZnSO₄: No reaction! Copper cannot displace more reactive metals.
