📚 Archimedes' Principle & Law of Flotation

1. Archimedes' Principle (c. 250 BC)

When a body is partially or completely immersed in a fluid at rest, it experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by the body:

Fb = mdisplaced × g = ρfluid × Vsubmerged × g

2. The Law of Flotation

For a body floating in equilibrium, the upward buoyant force equals the downward weight of the body ($F_b = F_g$):

ρfluid × Vsub × g = ρobj × Vtotal × g
⇒ Vsub / Vtotal = ρobj / ρfluid
  • Case 1 (ρobj < ρfluid): The body floats with fraction $\frac{\rho_{\text{obj}}}{\rho_{\text{fluid}}}$ submerged (e.g. ice in water: $\frac{0.92}{1.00} = 92\%$ submerged).
  • Case 2 (ρobj = ρfluid): Neutral buoyancy; remains suspended at any submerged depth.
  • Case 3 (ρobj > ρfluid): The body sinks to the bottom because downward gravity exceeds maximum buoyant force ($F_g > F_{b,\max}$).
