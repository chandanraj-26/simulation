📚 Projectile Motion Theory & Derivations

1. Velocity Decomposition

A projectile moves under constant downward gravitational acceleration ($a_y = -g$) with zero horizontal acceleration ($a_x = 0$ in vacuum). The initial velocity decomposes into orthogonal components:

v_0x = v₀ · cos(θ)
v_0y = v₀ · sin(θ)

2. Peak Height Derivation

At maximum height, vertical velocity momentarily reaches zero ($v_y = 0$):

v_y² = v_0y² - 2g(H - y₀) ⟹ H_max = y₀ + (v₀² · sin²(θ)) / (2g)

3. Time of Flight & Maximum Range

For a flat launch surface ($y_0 = 0$):

t_flight = 2 · v_0y / g = (2v₀ · sin(θ)) / g
R = v_0x · t_flight = (v₀² · 2 sin(θ) cos(θ)) / g = (v₀² · sin(2θ)) / g

Since $\sin(2\theta)$ has a maximum value of $1$ when $2\theta = 90^\circ$, maximum horizontal range always occurs at θ = 45°!

4. Complementary Angles Symmetry

Because $\sin(2(90^\circ - \theta)) = \sin(180^\circ - 2\theta) = \sin(2\theta)$, any pair of complementary launch angles (such as $30^\circ$ and $60^\circ$, or $15^\circ$ and $75^\circ$) produces the exact same landing range!
