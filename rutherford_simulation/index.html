📖 Laboratory Manual: Rutherford Alpha Scattering

1. Historical Background: In 1911, Ernest Rutherford (with Hans Geiger and Ernest Marsden) bombarded a 100 nm thin gold foil with energetic alpha particles ($\text{He}^{2+}$, $+2e$).


2. The Three Landmark Observations:

  • Most particles (> 99%) passed straight through without any deflection $\implies$ Most of the atom is completely empty space.
  • A few particles were deflected by small to moderate angles $\implies$ There must be a concentrated positive charge inside the atom.
  • Surprisingly, 1 in about 12,000 particles rebounded backwards ($\theta \ge 90^\circ$) $\implies$ All the positive charge and almost all the mass of the atom is concentrated in an unimaginably tiny, dense nucleus at the center.

3. Nuclear vs. Atomic Scale: The radius of an atom is $\approx 10^{-10}\text{ m}$ ($1\text{ \AA}$), while the radius of the nucleus is only $\approx 10^{-15}\text{ to } 10^{-14}\text{ m}$ (about 10,000 to 100,000 times smaller!).
