📖 Osmosis & Water Potential Theory

1. Osmosis & Semi-Permeable Membranes

Osmosis is the net diffusion of water molecules across a selectively permeable membrane from a region of higher water potential (lower solute concentration) to a region of lower water potential (higher solute concentration).

2. Water Potential Equation (Ψ = Ψs + Ψp)
Ψ = Ψs + Ψp

Ψs (Solute Potential): Always zero or negative. Pure water has Ψs = 0 MPa. Adding solute decreases Ψs according to the van 't Hoff relation: Ψs = -iCRT.

Ψp (Pressure Potential / Turgor): Physical hydrostatic pressure exerted by the rigid cellulose cell wall against the swollen protoplast. In animal cells (like RBCs), Ψp = 0 because they lack a cell wall.

3. Plasmolysis vs. Cytolysis (Hemolysis)

Plant Cell: In hypotonic solution, water rushes in until turgor pressure (Ψp) balances solute potential (turgid state). In hypertonic solution, water leaves, shrinking the protoplast away from the wall (plasmolysis). Returning it to pure water reverses this (deplasmolysis).

Animal Cell (RBC): Without a rigid wall, red blood cells swell and undergo membrane rupture (hemolysis) in hypotonic solutions (≤ 0.2% solute). In hypertonic solutions, they shrink and shrivel (crenation).

4. Aquaporin Water Channels

Aquaporins are membrane channel proteins discovered by Peter Agre (Nobel Prize in Chemistry, 2003) that facilitate rapid, single-file water flux through biological membranes without allowing ions or large solutes to pass.
