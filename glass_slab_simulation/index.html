Refraction Through Glass Slab — NCERT Theory

1. First Refraction (Air → Glass):
When a light ray enters from an optically rarer medium (air, n1 ≈ 1.0) into a denser medium (glass, n2 > 1.0), it slows down and bends towards the normal (r < i) obeying Snell's Law:

Snell's Law:
n1 · sin(i) = n2 · sin(r)

2. Second Refraction (Glass → Air):
At the lower boundary, light exits the glass back into air. It speeds up and bends away from the normal (e > r). Because opposite slab faces are strictly parallel, the angle of incidence on the lower surface equals the angle of refraction at the upper surface (r1 = r2 = r), which proves that the emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray:

Emergence Angle:
e = i   (Emergent ray ∥ Incident ray)

3. Lateral Displacement (d):
The perpendicular separation between the original path of the incident ray and the emergent ray is called Lateral Displacement (d):

Lateral Shift Equation:
d = [ t · sin(i − r) ] / cos(r)

Lateral displacement increases with:

  • Thickness of the glass slab (t)
  • Refractive index of the medium (n)
  • Angle of incidence (i)
