📚 Atomic Emission Spectroscopy Theory

1. The Physics of Flame Colors

When a metal salt is introduced into the high-temperature zone of a Bunsen burner, thermal kinetic energy vaporizes and dissociates the salt into gaseous metal atoms and cations. Valence electrons absorb discrete quanta of energy and undergo promotion from ground state orbitals to higher energy excited orbitals:

Atom (Ground State) + Thermal Energy → Atom* (Excited State)

2. Quantum Photon Emission (Planck-Einstein Equation)

The excited state is unstable and has a lifetime of mere nanoseconds. As the electron relaxes back down to a lower energy level, it releases the excess energy as a single photon of electromagnetic radiation:

ΔE = E₂ - E₁ = hν = (h · c) / λ
  • h = Planck's Constant ($6.626 \times 10^{-34}\text{ J}\cdot\text{s}$)
  • c = Speed of Light ($3.00 \times 10^{8}\text{ m/s}$)
  • λ = Wavelength of emitted light in nanometers (nm)
  • ΔE = Energy transition in electronvolts (eV)

3. Role of Cobalt Blue Glass

Sodium is an exceptionally ubiquitous element found in glassware, dust, and human skin oils. Because its 589 nm D-line emission is intensely brilliant, even trace sodium contaminants mask weaker violet potassium emissions. Cobalt blue glass contains $\text{Co}^{2+}$ ions that absorb yellow light ($580–600\text{ nm}$), transmitting only blue, violet, and deep red.
