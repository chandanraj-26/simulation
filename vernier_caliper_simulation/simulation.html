📚 Metrology Principles & Least Count Formulas

1. Vernier Caliper Principles

Invented by Pierre Vernier (1631), this instrument achieves precision beyond direct eye resolution by aligning two scales of slightly different division lengths.

1 MSD = 1 mm
10 Vernier Divisions = 9 MSD = 9 mm ⇒ 1 VSD = 0.9 mm
Least Count (LC) = 1 MSD − 1 VSD = 1.0 − 0.9 = 0.1 mm (0.01 cm)

To read: Identify the last main scale division before the Vernier zero (MSR). Then find which Vernier division mark lines up directly with any main scale mark (VSR).

2. Micrometer Screw Gauge Principles

Utilizes the principle of the micrometer screw: converting rotational motion along a precision helical thread into minute axial displacement.

Pitch = Linear distance moved per 1 complete rotation = 1.0 mm
Circular Scale Divisions = 100
Least Count (LC) = Pitch / Divisions = 1.0 mm / 100 = 0.01 mm (10 μm)

3. Zero Error Correction

When the measuring jaws or anvil and spindle touch with no object present:

  • Nil Zero Error: Zero of vernier/circular scale aligns perfectly with main zero/datum line.
  • Positive Zero Error (+e): Vernier/circular zero is ahead of the reference datum. The instrument overreads, so subtract $e$.
  • Negative Zero Error (−e): Vernier/circular zero is behind the reference datum. The instrument underreads, so add $|e|$.
Corrected Reading = Observed Reading − (± Zero Error)
