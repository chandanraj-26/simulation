Einstein's Photoelectric Equation — Theoretical Principles

1. Discovery & Wave Theory Failure

Classical wave theory predicted that electron emission should occur at any light frequency if the intensity was high enough, with a noticeable time lag. Experiments by Hertz, Hallwachs, and Lenard revealed that:

  • Emission is completely instantaneous (< 10⁻⁹ s).
  • No electrons are emitted below a specific threshold frequency (ν₀), regardless of intensity.
  • Maximum kinetic energy is independent of light intensity and depends solely on frequency.

2. Einstein's Photon Theory (1905)

Albert Einstein explained the effect by proposing that light consists of discrete energy quanta called photons, each carrying energy:

E = h · ν = (h · c) / λ

When a photon of energy E strikes a conduction electron, it transfers all its energy instantaneously in a 1-to-1 interaction. Part of the energy overcomes the metal binding barrier (Work Function, Φ₀), and the remaining energy becomes electron kinetic energy:

K_{max} = hν − Φ₀ = e · V₀

3. Stopping Potential (V₀) & Determination of Planck's Constant

By applying a negative (retarding) voltage to the collector plate, electrons are decelerated. The exact potential required to reduce photocurrent to zero is the stopping potential (V₀):

V₀ = (h / e) · ν − (Φ₀ / e)

Plotting V₀ against frequency ν yields a straight line with:

  • Slope: equal to h / e ≈ 4.136 × 10⁻¹⁵ V·s (universal constant for all metals!).
  • X-intercept: equal to the material's threshold frequency ν₀ = Φ₀ / h.
  • Y-intercept: equal to negative work function potential −Φ₀ / e.

4. Laws of Photoelectric Emission

  1. For a given frequency (ν > ν₀), the number of photoelectrons emitted per second (saturation photocurrent) is directly proportional to the incident light intensity.
  2. The maximum kinetic energy and stopping potential increase linearly with frequency and are completely independent of intensity.
  3. Emission occurs only when incident frequency exceeds threshold frequency (ν ≥ ν₀).
