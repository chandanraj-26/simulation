📚 Stomatal Transpiration & Guard Cell Osmoregulation

1. Guard Cell Anatomy (Reniform Dicot Stomata)

Each stomatal pore is flanked by two specialized, photosynthetic guard cells. Their cell walls have an anisotropic construction:

  • Inner Wall (facing pore): Very thick, inelastic, and reinforced with radial cellulose microfibrils.
  • Outer Wall (facing subsidiary cells): Thin and highly stretchable.

2. Potassium Ion (K⁺) Mechanism (Levitt, 1974)

Daytime Opening:
Light ⇒ Proton pump (H⁺-ATPase) exports H⁺
⇒ Hyperpolarization drives massive K⁺ influx from subsidiary cells
⇒ Water potential (Ψw) drops ⇒ Water rushes in via endosmosis
⇒ Turgor pressure (Ψp) surges ⇒ Thin outer walls bulge outward, pulling thick inner walls apart ⇒ PORE OPENS.
Nighttime / Drought Closure:
Darkness or Abscisic Acid (ABA) ⇒ Calcium signaling triggers K⁺ efflux
⇒ Water potential rises ⇒ Exosmosis of water
⇒ Guard cells become flaccid ⇒ PORE CLOSES to conserve water.
