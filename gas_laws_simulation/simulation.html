📚 Ideal Gas Laws & Kinetic Theory

1. The Ideal Gas Equation

The microscopic state of an ideal gas is unified by the empirical Ideal Gas Equation:

P · V = n · R · T
  • P = Pressure in atmospheres (atm) or Pascals (Pa)
  • V = Volume in Liters (L) or cubic meters (m³)
  • n = Amount of substance in moles (mol)
  • R = Universal Gas Constant ($0.08206\text{ L}\cdot\text{atm/(mol}\cdot\text{K)}$ or $8.314\text{ J/(mol}\cdot\text{K)}$)
  • T = Absolute temperature in Kelvin (K = °C + 273.15)

2. The Four Classical Gas Laws

  • Boyle’s Law (1662): At constant $T$ and $n$, $P_1 V_1 = P_2 V_2 \implies P \propto 1/V$. Compressing gas to half its volume doubles the collision frequency and pressure.
  • Charles’s Law (1787): At constant $P$ and $n$, $V_1 / T_1 = V_2 / T_2 \implies V \propto T$. Gas volume extrapolates directly to zero at Absolute Zero ($0\text{ K} = -273.15^\circ\text{C}$).
  • Gay-Lussac’s Law (1802): At constant $V$ and $n$, $P_1 / T_1 = P_2 / T_2 \implies P \propto T$. Heating a rigid closed cylinder causes proportional pressure rise.
  • Avogadro’s Hypothesis (1811): At constant $P$ and $T$, $V_1 / n_1 = V_2 / n_2 \implies V \propto n$. Equal volumes contain equal numbers of molecules.

3. Maxwell-Boltzmann Speed Distribution

Gas molecules do not all travel at the same velocity; thermal collisions produce a statistical speed distribution:

v_rms = √( 3 · R · T / M )

At the same temperature, lighter molecules (such as Helium, $M = 4$) travel far faster than heavy molecules (Argon, $M = 40$), spreading out the velocity histogram to higher speeds.
