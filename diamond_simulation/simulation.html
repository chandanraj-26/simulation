📚 Snell's Law, Total Internal Reflection & Tolkowsky Cut Theory
1. Snell's Law of Refraction

When light passes from one medium (e.g., air with index $n_1$) into a denser optical medium (e.g., diamond with index $n_2$), it bends toward the normal line:

n₁ · sin(θ₁) = n₂ · sin(θ₂)  &implies;  sin(θ₂) = (n₁ / n₂) · sin(θ₁)

Because diamond has an extraordinarily high refractive index ($n \approx 2.417$), light slows down dramatically from $300,000\text{ km/s}$ in vacuum to only $124,000\text{ km/s}$ inside diamond, bending sharply.

2. Total Internal Reflection (TIR) & Critical Angle

When light inside diamond strikes an internal facet attempting to exit into air, if the incident angle exceeds the critical angle $\theta_c$, 100% of the light is reflected internally with zero transmission:

θ_c = arcsin(n_air / n_gem) = arcsin(1.0 / 2.417) ≈ 24.4°

Compare this to ordinary crown glass ($n = 1.52$, $\theta_c \approx 41.1^\circ$). Diamond's tiny critical angle ($24.4^\circ$) means light entering a diamond is far more easily trapped and bounced internally before exiting through the top!

3. Cauchy's Chromatic Dispersion & Fire

The refractive index is not constant — it depends on wavelength $\lambda$. Violet light bends more than red light:

n(λ) = n_D + B · (1 / λ² - 1 / λ_D²)

This differential bending separates white light into a dazzling spectrum (VIBGYOR). In gemology, this spectral flare is called "Fire".

4. Marcel Tolkowsky's 1919 Ideal Brilliant Cut

In 1919, mathematician and gemologist Marcel Tolkowsky calculated the exact proportions needed for light entering through the table facet to bounce off both pavilion facets via Total Internal Reflection and exit back up through the crown facets to the viewer's eyes:

  • Crown Angle: $34.5^\circ$
  • Pavilion Angle: $40.75^\circ$ (critical sweet spot!)
  • Table Diameter: $57\%$ of total girdle width
