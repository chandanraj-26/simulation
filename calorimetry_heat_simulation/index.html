Calorimetry & Thermochemistry — NCERT Theory

1. First Law of Thermodynamics in Calorimetry:
In an isolated calorimeter, assuming negligible heat loss to the environment, conservation of energy dictates:

qreaction + qsolution = 0  ⇒  qreaction = −m · c · ΔT
Where m = solution mass (g), c = 4.184 J/(g·°C), ΔT = Tfinal − Tinitial

2. Exothermic Dissolution:
Lattice dissociation energy is smaller than hydration enthalpy. Net thermal energy is released into aqueous solution:

Tf > Ti   |   ΔHsol < 0 (Heat Released)
Example: CaCl₂ + H₂O → Ca²⁺(aq) + 2Cl⁻(aq) + Heat (ΔH = −81.3 kJ/mol)

3. Endothermic Dissolution:
Lattice energy exceeds hydration enthalpy. Thermal kinetic energy is absorbed from the solvent molecules, causing the solution temperature to drop:

Tf < Ti   |   ΔHsol > 0 (Heat Absorbed)
Example: NH₄NO₃ + H₂O + Heat → NH₄⁺(aq) + NO₃⁻(aq) (ΔH = +25.7 kJ/mol)
