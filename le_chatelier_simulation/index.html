Le Chatelier's Principle — Theoretical Principles

1. Le Chatelier's Principle (1884)

If a dynamic chemical equilibrium is subjected to a change in concentration, temperature, or pressure, the system readjusts itself in a direction that opposes the applied disturbance.

2. System 1: Iron(III) Thiocyanate Equilibrium

Fe³⁺(aq) + SCN⁻(aq) ⇌ [Fe(SCN)]²⁺(aq)   (ΔH < 0, Exothermic)
  • Increasing [Fe³⁺] or [SCN⁻]: System consumes added reactants by shifting forward, increasing blood-red complex concentration.
  • Adding Oxalic Acid: C₂O₄²⁻ ions strongly bind Fe³⁺ into stable $[\text{Fe(C}_2\text{O}_4)_3]^{3-}$, decreasing free $[\text{Fe}^{3+}]$, shifting equilibrium backward into the pale yellow state.
  • Adding AgNO₃: Ag⁺ ions precipitate insoluble $\text{AgSCN}\downarrow$, removing SCN⁻ and shifting equilibrium backward.
  • Temperature Effect: Since the forward reaction is exothermic, heating (Tube 3) shifts backward (fainter color), whereas cooling in an ice bath (Tube 4) shifts forward (deeper red).

3. System 2: Cobalt(II) Chloro Complex Equilibrium

[Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺(aq) [Pink] + 4Cl⁻(aq) ⇌ [CoCl₄]²⁻(aq) [Blue] + 6H₂O(l)   (ΔH > 0, Endothermic)
  • Temperature Effect: The forward reaction absorbs heat ($\Delta H > 0$). In a hot water bath ($80^\circ\text{C}$, Tube 3), equilibrium shifts forward to form intense royal-blue $[\text{CoCl}_4]^{2-}$. In an ice bath ($0^\circ\text{C}$, Tube 4), it shifts backward to pink $[\text{Co(H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+}$.
  • Adding Conc. HCl: Increases $[\text{Cl}^-]$, driving equilibrium forward to produce royal blue.
  • Dilution with Water: Favors the side with more water molecules (reverse), turning pink.
