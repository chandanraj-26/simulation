📚 Roller Coaster Physics & The 2.5R Rule

1. Conservation of Mechanical Energy

At any point on the track at vertical height $y$, the cart’s total mechanical energy is the sum of its Gravitational Potential Energy ($PE$) and Kinetic Energy ($KE$):

E_total = PE + KE = m·g·y + ½·m·v² = m·g·h₀

Solving for the speed as a function of drop height:

v(y) = √( 2·g·(h₀ - y) )

Notice that the cart's speed is completely independent of its mass $m$!

2. Centripetal Force & Normal Force at the Apex

When the cart traverses a loop of radius $R$, the track must exert an inward contact force (Normal Force $N$) toward the center. At the top of the loop (apex at $y = 2R$), both gravity $mg$ and the normal force $N$ point downwards:

N + m·g = m·v² / R ⟹ N = m·( v² / R - g )

3. The 2.5R Rule Derivation

To prevent the cart from falling off the rails, the contact force cannot drop below zero ($N \ge 0$). This establishes the minimum apex speed:

v_apex ≥ √( g·R )

By energy conservation from release height $h_0$ to apex height $y = 2R$:

m·g·h_min = m·g·(2R) + ½·m·(√(gR))² = 2·m·g·R + 0.5·m·g·R = 2.5·m·g·R
⟹ h_min = 2.5 · R

The 2.5R Rule: The initial hill must be at least 2.5 times the radius of a circular loop. If friction or air resistance is present, it must be even higher!

4. The "Loop Fail" Phenomenon

When $h_0 < 2.5R$, the speed at the upper loop is too low to sustain circular motion ($v^2/R < g$). The normal contact force drops to zero ($N = 0$). The cart lifts off the track rails and transitions into free-fall ballistic projectile motion under gravity ($a_x = 0, a_y = -g$).

5. Why Real Coasters Use "Clothoid" Loops

A perfectly circular loop requires an abrupt change in curvature at the entrance, generating dangerous, spine-compressing G-forces (> 6–8 Gs). Real roller coasters use teardrop-shaped loops (clothoids or Euler spirals) where the radius of curvature decreases gradually toward the top, keeping entry forces comfortable (≤ 3.5–4 Gs).
