Agarose Gel Electrophoresis — Theoretical Principles

1. Principle of DNA Electrophoresis

DNA molecules possess a uniform negative electrical charge across their entire length due to the repeating negatively charged phosphate groups ($\text{PO}_4^{3-}$) along their sugar-phosphate backbone. When placed in an electric field, DNA fragments migrate toward the positive electrode (Anode, +):

Migration Direction: Cathode (−) → Anode (+)

2. Molecular Sieving Through Agarose Mesh

Agarose forms a three-dimensional porous polymer network. Rather than separating by charge-to-mass ratio (which is virtually identical for all double-stranded DNA), separation occurs strictly via molecular sieving:

  • Smaller DNA fragments slip easily through polymer pores and travel much faster/farther.
  • Larger fragments experience severe frictional hindrance and migrate slowly near the top.

The relative migration distance d is inversely proportional to the logarithm of the fragment size (in base pairs, bp):

d ∝ 1 / log₁₀(Base Pairs)

3. DNA Ladder Reference Standards

A calibrated mixture of DNA fragments of known molecular weights (such as a 1 kb ladder) is run in Lane 1 alongside unknown samples. Plotting migration distance d against $\log_{10}(\text{bp})$ creates a linear calibration curve from which unknown fragment sizes can be deduced with high precision.

4. UV Transillumination & Intercalating Dyes

Because DNA is colorless and transparent in solution, an intercalating fluorescent dye such as Ethidium Bromide (EtBr) or GelGreen is incorporated into the gel:

  • The planar dye molecule slips between stacked DNA base pairs.
  • When irradiated with ultraviolet light ($\approx 302\text{–}365\text{ nm}$), the intercalated dye absorbs UV and fluoresces brightly in the visible spectrum ($\lambda \approx 590\text{ nm}$ orange or $530\text{ nm}$ green), revealing the discrete DNA bands.

5. DNA Fingerprinting & Forensic Matching

Forensic identification relies on hypervariable VNTRs (Variable Number Tandem Repeats) or STRs (Short Tandem Repeats) cut by specific restriction endonucleases. If the banding pattern of a suspect's DNA matches the crime scene evidence across multiple loci (as seen in Suspect 2), the probability of identity approaches certainty.
