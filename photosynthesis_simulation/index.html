📖 Photosynthesis & Limiting Factors Theory

1. Overall Photosynthesis Reaction
6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + Light Energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ (Glucose) + 6 O₂ ↑

Oxygen gas released in the waterweed bubbler originates entirely from the photolysis (splitting) of water molecules in the thylakoid lumen during the light-dependent stage.

2. Inverse-Square Law of Light (I ∝ 1/d²)
I = I₀ / (d / d₀)²

As the distance ($d$) between the lamp and the Elodea increases, light intensity drops geometrically. Halving the distance quadruples the light intensity reaching the chloroplasts.

3. Action Spectrum & The Green Light Paradox

Chlorophyll pigments ($a$ and $b$) absorb light primarily in the blue (~450 nm) and red (~660 nm) regions. Green light (~530 nm) is largely reflected or transmitted, producing very few oxygen bubbles despite high illumination.

4. Blackman's Principle of Limiting Factors

"When a process is conditioned as to its rapidity by a number of separate factors, the rate of the process is limited by the pace of the slowest factor." — F.F. Blackman (1905).

At low carbon dioxide (0% NaHCO₃) or extreme temperatures (>42°C enzyme denaturation), increasing light intensity cannot increase the rate of photosynthesis.
