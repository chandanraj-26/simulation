    📚 Mitosis & Microscopy Cytology Guide

    1. The Stages of Mitosis (Cell Division)

    1. Interphase (G1, S, G2): Metabolic growth and complete DNA replication. The nucleus is bounded by an intact membrane with a prominent nucleolus; chromatin is diffuse.
    2. Prophase: Chromatin supercoils into visible condensed chromosomes (paired sister chromatids). The nucleolus and nuclear envelope disassemble. Spindle fibers begin forming.
    3. Metaphase: Chromosomes reach peak condensation and align along the central equatorial Metaphase Plate. Bipolar kinetochore microtubules attach under mechanical tension.
    4. Anaphase: Cohesin protein complexes are cleaved by Separase. Sister chromatids separate and are pulled toward opposite spindle poles by shortening kinetochore microtubules, forming characteristic V-shapes.
    5. Telophase & Cytokinesis: Nuclear envelopes reassemble around daughter chromosome clusters. In plant cells (*Allium cepa*), a Phragmoplast guides vesicle fusion to build a primary Cell Plate. In animal cells (*Whitefish*), a contractile ring constricts a Cleavage Furrow.

    2. Calculating the Mitotic Index (MI)

    The Mitotic Index quantifies the proliferative activity of a tissue (such as apical meristems or tumors):

    $$\text{Mitotic Index} = \frac{\text{Number of Dividing Cells (P + M + A + T)}}{\text{Total Number of Cells Counted}} \times 100\%$$

    3. Microscope Optical Physics & Oil Immersion

    • Total Magnification: $M_{\text{total}} = M_{\text{ocular}} \times M_{\text{objective}} = 10\times \times 40\times = 400\times$.
    • Numerical Aperture (NA) & Resolution Limit: $d = \frac{0.61 \lambda}{\text{NA}} = \frac{0.61 \lambda}{n \sin \theta}$.
    • Immersion Oil Principle: At 100× magnification, the narrow gap between glass cover slip and objective lens causes light to refract away in air ($n = 1.000$). Cedarwood or synthetic immersion oil matches the refractive index of glass ($n = 1.515$), preventing light refraction loss and dramatically boosting resolution!
