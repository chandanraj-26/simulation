📚 Cardiac Cycle & Wiggers Diagram Reference

1. The 7 Classical Phases of the Cardiac Cycle

  1. Atrial Systole: SA node fires (P wave); atria contract, boosting ventricular volume by 15–20% (the "atrial kick"). End-Diastolic Volume ($EDV \approx 120\text{–}130\text{ mL}$) reached.
  2. Isovolumetric Contraction: QRS triggers ventricular excitation. Pressure skyrockets; AV valves (Tricuspid & Mitral) snap shut producing the $S_1$ ("LUB") heart sound. All 4 valves are closed.
  3. Rapid Ventricular Ejection: Ventricular pressure surpasses aortic pressure (80 mmHg); Semilunar valves open, briskly expelling ~70% of the stroke volume.
  4. Reduced Ventricular Ejection: Ventricles begin repolarizing (T wave); blood outflow slows as ventricular pressure declines.
  5. Isovolumetric Relaxation: Ventricular pressure drops below aortic/pulmonic pressure; Semilunar valves snap shut producing the $S_2$ ("DUB") sound and the aortic Dicrotic Notch (Incisura). All 4 valves closed.
  6. Rapid Ventricular Filling: Ventricular pressure falls below atrial pressure; AV valves open, and passive ventricular filling rapidly surges.
  7. Diastasis (Reduced Inflow): Slower passive blood inflow as atria and ventricles equalize before the next SA nodal impulse.

2. Fundamental Clinical Equations

  • Stroke Volume ($SV$): $SV = EDV - ESV \approx 125 - 55 = 70\text{ mL}$
  • Cardiac Output ($CO$): $CO = HR \times SV = 72 \times 70\text{ mL} \approx 5.04\text{ L/min}$
  • Ejection Fraction ($EF$): $EF = \frac{SV}{EDV} \times 100\% \approx 55\text{–}65\%$
  • Mean Arterial Pressure ($MAP$): $MAP = DP + \frac{1}{3}(SP - DP) \approx 80 + \frac{1}{3}(40) = 93.3\text{ mmHg}$

3. Heart Sounds & Auscultation

  • $S_1$ ("LUB"): Caused by closure of Mitral & Tricuspid valves at the start of systole. Loudest at the Cardiac Apex (Mitral Area).
  • $S_2$ ("DUB"): Caused by closure of Aortic & Pulmonary valves at the start of diastole. Loudest at the Base of the heart (Aortic & Pulmonic Areas).
  • Aortic Stenosis: Systolic ejection diamond-shaped murmur caused by turbulent jetting across a calcified/narrowed aortic valve.
  • Mitral Regurgitation: Holosystolic blowing murmur extending from $S_1$ to $S_2$ caused by backward leakage into the left atrium during ventricular contraction.
