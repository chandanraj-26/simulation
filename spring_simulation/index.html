📚 Hooke's Law & Harmonic Motion Theory

1. Hooke's Law of Elasticity

Discovered by Robert Hooke in 1660, Hooke's law states that the restoring force exerted by an ideal spring is directly proportional to its displacement from equilibrium ($\Delta x$):

F_s = -k · Δx

where $k$ is the spring constant (stiffness) measured in $\text{N/m}$. The negative sign indicates that the restoring force always opposes displacement.

2. Static Equilibrium & Spring Balance Scale

When a mass $m$ hangs at rest under gravity $g$, the downward weight ($W = mg$) is exactly balanced by the upward spring tension ($F_s = k \Delta x_{\text{eq}}$):

k · Δx_eq = m · g ⟹ m = (k · Δx_eq) / g

This is the fundamental principle of everyday spring scales! By measuring $\Delta x$, you directly determine unknown mass $m$.

3. Series vs. Parallel Spring Combinations

Parallel: k_eff = k₁ + k₂ (Stiffer system, halves elongation)
Series: 1 / k_eff = 1 / k₁ + 1 / k₂ ⟹ k_eff = (k₁ · k₂) / (k₁ + k₂) (More flexible)

4. Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM) Period

When displaced from equilibrium and released, the mass oscillates in simple harmonic motion:

ω₀ = √(k / m) ⟹ T = 2π / ω₀ = 2π · √(m / k)

Notice that the period $T$ depends only on mass $m$ and spring stiffness $k$ — it is completely independent of gravitational acceleration $g$!
