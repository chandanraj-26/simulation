📚 Archimedes’ Principle & Hydrostatics Theory

1. The Principle of Archimedes

Formulated by Archimedes of Syracuse in the 3rd century BCE:

"Any body wholly or partially submerged in a fluid experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by the body."

F_B = m_fluid · g = ρ_fluid · V_submerged · g

2. Hydrostatic Origin of Buoyancy

Buoyant force is not a mysterious upward suction; it is the net resultant vector of hydrostatic pressure forces acting over the entire surface area of the immersed body.

Since fluid pressure increases linearly with depth according to:

P(h) = P₀ + ρ_fluid · g · h

The bottom surface of the object is at a greater depth ($h_2$) than the top surface ($h_1$). Consequently, the upward pressure against the bottom exceeds the downward pressure on the top:

ΔP = P_bottom - P_top = ρ_fluid · g · (h₂ - h₁) = ρ_fluid · g · H

F_buoyant = ΔP · Area = ρ_fluid · g · (H · Area) = ρ_fluid · g · V_submerged

Lateral pressure forces acting on opposite sides cancel each other out completely due to symmetry, leaving only a net vertical upward force!

3. Apparent Weight Loss

When an object is weighed while immersed in a fluid using a spring balance, the scale reads the string tension $T$:

T = W_apparent = W_true - F_B

Loss in Weight = W_true - W_apparent = F_B = W_displaced fluid

In the classic Eureka Can experiment, water displaced through the overflow spout is collected in a graduated cylinder. Its measured weight identically matches the apparent loss in weight measured by the spring balance.

4. The Law of Flotation

A freely floating body sinks until the weight of the fluid it displaces equals its own total weight:

W_object = F_B &implies; (ρ_object · V_total · g) = (ρ_fluid · V_submerged · g)

Fraction Submerged = V_submerged / V_total = ρ_object / ρ_fluid

- Floating: $\rho_{\text{object}} < \rho_{\text{fluid}}$ (e.g. ice in water, 91.7% submerged)
- Neutral Buoyancy: $\rho_{\text{object}} = \rho_{\text{fluid}}$ (suspends without sinking or rising)
- Sinking: $\rho_{\text{object}} > \rho_{\text{fluid}}$ (sinks to the container floor)

👑 The Mystery of King Hiero's Crown

Archimedes' Famous "Eureka!" Discovery

King Hiero II of Syracuse gave a goldsmith a specific weight of pure gold to make a sacred votive crown. Suspecting that the goldsmith had kept some gold and substituted an equal mass of cheaper silver, Hiero challenged Archimedes to determine if the crown was pure gold—without damaging the crown!

While stepping into a full public bath, Archimedes noticed the water spilling over the edge. In a flash of genius, he realized that a crown adulterated with silver would have a larger volume (because silver is less dense than gold, $\rho_{\text{Ag}} \approx 10500\text{ kg/m}^3$ vs $\rho_{\text{Au}} = 19300\text{ kg/m}^3$) and would therefore displace more water!

Case A: Pure Gold Crown

Density: 19,300 kg/m³
Volume: Smallest
Displaced Water: Minimal
Weight Loss: Minimal

Case B: Goldsmith's Alloy Crown

Density: 13,800 kg/m³ (Gold + Silver)
Volume: 39.8% Larger
Displaced Water: Significantly Greater!
Weight Loss: Much Higher!

💡 Experiment Tip: In Eureka Overflow mode, observe the reading on the catch beaker scale when you immerse each crown. The alloy crown displaces noticeably more water, exposing the fraud!
