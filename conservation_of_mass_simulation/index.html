Lavoisier's Principle: In any closed chemical reaction, the total mass of the products equals the total mass of the reactants ($m_{\text{reactants}} = m_{\text{products}}$).

Laboratory Quests (NCERT)

📖 Laboratory Manual: Law of Conservation of Mass

1. Historical Context: Formulated by French chemist Antoine Lavoisier in 1789. The law states that mass is neither created nor destroyed during a chemical reaction.


2. NCERT Activity 3.1 Procedure:

  • Take $10\text{ mL}$ of Solution Y in a small ignition tube and suspend it inside a conical flask containing $40\text{ mL}$ of Solution X.
  • Put a rubber cork tightly on the mouth of the flask so that no gas or liquid escapes.
  • Weigh the whole flask assembly with precision balance: record mass $m_1$.
  • Now tilt and swirl the flask so that the solutions X and Y mix together.
  • Weigh the flask again: record mass $m_2$. You will find that $m_2 = m_1$.

3. Open vs Closed System (Activity 3.2): If the reaction produces a gas (like $\text{CO}_2$ from $\text{CaCO}_3 + \text{HCl}$) and the cork is removed, the gas escapes into the room causing an apparent mass deficit. In a closed flask, the gas is trapped and mass is conserved.
