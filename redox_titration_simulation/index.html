Permanganometry & Redox Titration — Theoretical Principles

1. Principle of Permanganate Titration

Potassium permanganate (KMnO₄) is a powerful oxidizing agent in acidic medium. In dilute H₂SO₄, the purple MnO₄⁻ ion undergoes a 5-electron reduction to virtually colorless manganese(II) ion (Mn²⁺):

MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O   (E° = +1.51 V)

2. Titration of Mohr's Salt (Ferrous Ammonium Sulfate)

Mohr's salt provides stable Fe²⁺ ions that undergo 1-electron oxidation to Fe³⁺:

Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ + e⁻   (E° = +0.77 V)

Multiplying the oxidation half-reaction by 5 gives the net stoichiometric ionic equation:

5Fe²⁺ + MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ → 5Fe³⁺ + Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O

Therefore, 1 mole of KMnO₄ oxidizes exactly 5 moles of Fe²⁺.

3. Self-Indicating Equivalence Point

No external indicator is needed. As long as unreacted Fe²⁺ ions remain in the flask, incoming purple KMnO₄ is instantaneously reduced to colorless Mn²⁺. Once all Fe²⁺ is oxidized, a single excess drop of 0.02 M KMnO₄ imparts a distinct, permanent faint pink color to the solution lasting for at least 30 seconds.

4. Why Dilute H₂SO₄ Is Used (and Not HCl or HNO₃)

  • HCl cannot be used: KMnO₄ oxidizes chloride ions (Cl⁻) to poisonous chlorine gas (Cl₂), causing erroneous over-consumption of titrant.
  • HNO₃ cannot be used: Nitric acid is itself an oxidizing agent and would compete with KMnO₄ by oxidizing Fe²⁺.
  • Dilute H₂SO₄ is ideal: Sulfate ions (SO₄²⁻) are completely resistant to further oxidation by KMnO₄.

5. Potentiometric Curve & Nernst Equation

The redox potential before equivalence follows the Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ couple:

E = 0.771 + 0.0591 · log₁₀([Fe³⁺] / [Fe²⁺])

At equivalence, the potential jumps sharply through the weighted formal potential:

E_{eq} = (5 · E°_{Mn} + E°_{Fe}) / 6 ≈ 1.387 V
