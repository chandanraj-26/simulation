Newton's Third Law: Action & Reaction

How Do Rockets Fly into Space?

Rockets don't push against the ground or air! Watch the introductory lesson video below to observe how burning fuel expels exhaust gases downward with tremendous force (Faction), which by Newton's Third Law creates an equal and opposite reaction force (Freaction = Fthrust) pushing the rocket upward into space.

🔻
1. The Action Force (Faction)

High-speed combustion gases are accelerated and forced out through the rocket nozzle downwards.

🔺
2. The Reaction Force (Fthrust)

By Newton's Third Law, the escaping gas pushes back on the rocket with equal force in the opposite direction (upwards).

🚀
3. Net Force & Lift-off (Fnet = ma)

When upward thrust exceeds the rocket's weight (W = mg), positive net force accelerates the rocket into the sky!

⚖️ Newton's 1st Law: At Rest (Balanced) Atmosphere: Sea Level (100% Air Density · 15°C)
1. Weight: W = m · g = (3,000 kg) · (9.8 m/s²) = 29,400 N
2. Net Force: Fnet = Fthrust - W = 40,000 N - 29,400 N = +10,600 N (Upward)
3. Acceleration: a = Fnet / m = 10,600 N / 3,000 kg = +3.53 m/s²
Before launch, resting on the pad: Normal reaction from ground balances weight. Once thrust is applied, if thrust exceeds weight, net force is upward, producing positive acceleration.
