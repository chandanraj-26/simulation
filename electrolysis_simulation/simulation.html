📖 Laboratory Manual: Electrolysis & Electroplating

1. Electrolysis of Water: Pure water is a poor conductor. Adding a few drops of dilute $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ provides free $\text{H}^+$ and $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$ ions.


2. Stoichiometry (2:1 Ratio): For every 4 electrons transferred, 2 molecules of $\text{H}_2$ are formed at the cathode for every 1 molecule of $\text{O}_2$ formed at the anode. Thus, the volume of hydrogen gas collected is twice the volume of oxygen gas.


3. Gas Identification:

  • Hydrogen ($\text{H}_2$): Extinguishes a burning splint with a characteristic squeaky "POP".
  • Oxygen ($\text{O}_2$): Kindles a glowing splint into bright burning flame.

4. Copper Electroplating: Connect the pure copper plate to the positive terminal (anode) and the iron key to the negative terminal (cathode) in blue $\text{CuSO}_4$ solution. Copper dissolves at the anode and deposits onto the iron key.
