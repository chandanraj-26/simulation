📚 The pH Scale, Auto-Ionization & Dilution Principles

1. Definition of pH (S.P.L. Sørensen, 1909)

pH stands for "potenz" (power) of hydrogen ions. It is defined as the negative logarithm (base 10) of the molar hydronium ion concentration:

pH = −log₁₀[H⁺] = −log₁₀[H₃O⁺]
pOH = −log₁₀[OH⁻]

2. Auto-Ionization of Water & The Ionic Product (Kw)

Pure water self-ionizes in an equilibrium reaction:

2 H₂O(l) ⇌ H₃O⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq)
Kw = [H₃O⁺][OH⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ at 25°C
⇒ pH + pOH = 14.00

3. The Dilution Principle

When neutral distilled water (pH 7.0) is added to an acid, hydronium concentration decreases, causing pH to rise towards 7.0. It can never exceed 7.0 regardless of dilution extent. Conversely, diluting a base lowers hydroxide concentration, causing pH to fall towards 7.0.
