Magnetic Field Lines — NCERT Theory

1. Nature of Magnetic Field Lines:
Magnetic field lines are continuous closed loops. By international convention, outside the magnet they emerge from the North (N) pole and enter the South (S) pole, continuing internally from South back to North.


2. Dipole Field Strength & Variation:
The magnetic induction around a bar magnet of magnetic dipole moment M follows the inverse-cube law at distances r ≫ l:

Axial Field (End-on position):
Baxial = (μ₀ / 4π) · (2M / r³)

Equatorial Field (Broadside-on position):
Bequatorial = (μ₀ / 4π) · (M / r³)

Baxial = 2 · Bequatorial

3. Non-Intersection Law:
Two magnetic field lines can never cross each other. If they did, a compass placed at the intersection would have to point in two different directions at the same instant, which is physically impossible.


4. Neutral Points (Bnet = 0):
When placed in the Earth's magnetic field (horizontal component BH), specific locations emerge where the magnet's field is equal in magnitude and opposite in direction to BH:

Neutral Point Condition:
Bmagnet + BH = 0  ⇒  Bnet = 0
At neutral points, a magnetic compass experiences zero net torque and rests in arbitrary orientations.
