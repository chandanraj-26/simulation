📚 Modern Periodic Law & Chemical Periodicity Guide

🔬 Henry Moseley & Modern Periodic Law (1913)

Dmitri Mendeleev originally arranged elements in order of increasing atomic masses ($A$). However, Henry Moseley bombarded various elements with high-speed electrons and recorded the characteristic X-ray frequencies ($\nu$):

Moseley's Law: $\sqrt{\nu} = a(Z - b)$, where $Z$ is the atomic number.
Modern Periodic Law: "The physical and chemical properties of the elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers."

💡 Why Hinglish Mnemonics Work So Well

In competitive examinations like JEE Main, JEE Advanced, and NEET, instantaneous recall of group numbers, periods, and valence configurations is essential. Connecting arbitrary chemical symbols to vivid, humorous everyday Hinglish phrases creates strong neural associations:

• Group 2 Alkaline Earth Metals: Beta Mange Car Scooter Baap Raazi (Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba, Ra)
• Group 1 Alkali Metals: Helina Ki Rab Se Fariyad (H, Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, Fr)
• Group 13 Boron Family: Baingan Aloo Gajar In Thela (B, Al, Ga, In, Tl)
• Group 17 Halogens: Fir Kal Bahar Aayi Aunty (F, Cl, Br, I, At, Ts)

📈 Periodic Trends Across Periods & Down Groups

1. Atomic Radius: Decreases across a period (left to right) due to increasing effective nuclear charge ($Z_{\text{eff}}$) pulling electrons inward. Increases down a group due to the addition of principal electron shells ($n$).

2. Ionization Energy: Increases across a period as electrons are held more tightly. Decreases down a group due to increased atomic radius and electron shielding.

3. Electronegativity: Fluorine ($F, 3.98$) is the most electronegative element, followed by Oxygen ($O, 3.44$) and Nitrogen ($N, 3.04$). Caesium ($Cs, 0.79$) and Francium ($Fr, 0.7$) are the least electronegative.
