Galvanic Daniell Cell — Theoretical Principles

1. Half-Cell Electrochemistry

A Daniell cell converts spontaneous chemical redox energy into electrical energy using two half-cells:

  • Anode (Oxidation): Zinc strip immersed in ZnSO₄ solution. Zinc atoms lose electrons and enter solution as Zn²⁺ ions:
    Zn(s) → Zn²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻   (E°_{ox} = +0.763 V)
  • Cathode (Reduction): Copper strip immersed in CuSO₄ solution. Cu²⁺ ions in solution gain electrons and plate onto the copper strip:
    Cu²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻ → Cu(s)   (E°_{red} = +0.337 V)

Overall cell reaction:

Zn(s) + Cu²⁺(aq) → Zn²⁺(aq) + Cu(s)   (E°_{cell} = +1.100 V at 298 K)

2. Role of the Salt Bridge

The salt bridge is an inverted U-tube filled with an inert agar-agar gel containing strong electrolytes such as KCl or KNO₃ (chosen because K⁺ and Cl⁻ have nearly identical ionic mobilities, minimizing liquid junction potential):

  • Completes the internal electrical circuit by facilitating ion migration.
  • Maintains electrical neutrality: As Zn²⁺ accumulates at the anode, Cl⁻ ions flow into the zinc beaker. As Cu²⁺ is consumed at the cathode leaving excess SO₄²⁻, K⁺ ions flow into the copper beaker.
  • If the salt bridge is removed: Charges rapidly accumulate at both electrodes, creating an opposing back-EMF that immediately brings the current and cell voltage to zero.

3. The Nernst Equation

For non-standard concentrations, the electromotive force (EMF) is governed by the Nernst Equation:

E_{cell} = E°_{cell} − (2.303 · R · T) / (n · F) · log₁₀(Q)

At 25°C (298.15 K), with $n = 2$ and $Q = [\text{Zn}^{2+}] / [\text{Cu}^{2+}]$:

E_{cell} = 1.100 − (0.0591 / 2) · log₁₀([Zn²⁺] / [Cu²⁺])
  • Decreasing [Zn²⁺] or increasing [Cu²⁺] increases cell EMF ($E_{cell} > 1.10\text{ V}$).
  • Increasing [Zn²⁺] or decreasing [Cu²⁺] decreases cell EMF ($E_{cell} < 1.10\text{ V}$).

4. Thermodynamic Gibbs Free Energy (ΔG)

The electrical work done by the cell equals the decrease in Gibbs free energy:

ΔG = −n · F · E_{cell}

Since $E_{cell} > 0$, $\Delta G < 0$, confirming that the galvanic cell reaction proceeds spontaneously.
