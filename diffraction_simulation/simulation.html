Single-Slit Fraunhofer Diffraction — Theoretical Principles

1. Wavefront Division & Sinc² Intensity

When a monochromatic plane wavefront of wavelength λ strikes a rectangular slit of width a, every point on the slit acts as a secondary emitter of coherent cylindrical wavelets. Integrating these wavelets across the slit width gives the Fraunhofer intensity distribution:

I(θ) = I₀ · [sin(β) / β]²,   where β = (π · a · sinθ) / λ ≈ (π · a · y) / (λ · D)

2. Condition for Minima (Dark Bands)

Destructive interference occurs when the slit can be divided into an even number of equal halves that cancel pairwise, i.e., when $\beta = \pm m \pi$ ($m \neq 0$):

a · sinθ = ±m · λ ⇒ y_m = ± (m · λ · D) / a,   where m = 1, 2, 3, ...

3. Central Maximum Width (W₀)

The central maximum extends between the first minima on either side ($m = -1$ to $m = +1$):

  • Angular Half-Width: $\theta_0 = \lambda / a$ (in radians).
  • Linear Full Width on Screen:
    W₀ = 2 · y₁ = (2 · λ · D) / a

Notice that the central diffraction maximum is twice as wide as any of the secondary maxima ($W_{secondary} = \lambda D / a$).

4. Secondary Maxima & Intensity Falloff

Secondary maxima occur approximately halfway between minima, at $\beta \approx \pm 1.43\pi, \pm 2.46\pi, \dots$ Their peak intensities diminish rapidly:

  • Central Maximum ($m = 0$): $I = I_0$ (100%)
  • First Secondary Maximum: $I_1 \approx I_0 / (1.43\pi)^2 \approx 4.7\%$
  • Second Secondary Maximum: $I_2 \approx I_0 / (2.46\pi)^2 \approx 1.7\%$
