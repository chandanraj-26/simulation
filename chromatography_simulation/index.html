Separation Mechanism 🧪

Stationary Phase: Cellulose fibers with bound moisture forming a polar matrix.

Mobile Phase: Ascends via capillary action against gravity (Washburn kinetics).

Polarity Principle: Non-polar solutes partition into the mobile phase and travel faster (higher Rf), whereas polar solutes form hydrogen bonds with cellulose and migrate slower.

Chromatography Theory & Lab Protocol

1. Principle of Chromatography

Chromatography (from Greek chroma "color" and graphein "to write") is an analytical separation technique based on differential partitioning of solutes between a stationary phase and a mobile phase.

Retardation Factor: Rf = Distance traveled by solute / Distance traveled by solvent front

2. Plant Pigments in Spinach Extract

  • β-Carotene (Rf ≈ 0.94): Pure hydrocarbon carotenoid; non-polar, weakly adsorbed to paper, moves near the solvent front.
  • Xanthophyll (Rf ≈ 0.72): Oxygenated carotenoid containing polar hydroxyl (-OH) groups; travels slower than β-carotene.
  • Chlorophyll a (Rf ≈ 0.58): Blue-green pigment with a porphyrin ring and phytol tail.
  • Chlorophyll b (Rf ≈ 0.42): Yellow-green pigment with an aldehyde (-CHO) substituent in place of the methyl group in chlorophyll a, making it noticeably more polar and retained longer.

3. Capillary Ascent & Washburn Dynamics

The ascent of solvent through paper micro-capillaries obeys the Lucas-Washburn equation:

h(t) = √[ ( γ · r · cos θ / (2 · η) ) · t ]

Where γ is surface tension, η is solvent dynamic viscosity, r is average pore radius, and θ is the wetting contact angle. As the liquid front climbs, flow rate decreases due to viscous drag.

4. Ninhydrin Spraying for Amino Acids

Amino acids are colorless. After the solvent ascends, spraying with 0.2% ninhydrin reagent in ethanol and warm air drying converts free alpha-amino acids into Ruhemann's purple (diketohydrindylidene-diketohydrindamine) complexes with strong absorption at 570 nm.
