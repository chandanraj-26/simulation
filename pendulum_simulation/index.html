📚 Simple Pendulum & Isochronism Theory

1. Galileo's Discovery of Isochronism (1581)

While observing a bronze chandelier swinging in the Cathedral of Pisa, young Galileo Galilei used his own pulse to time its swings and discovered that for small angles, the period of a pendulum is independent of its amplitude!

2. Small-Angle Period Formula

Using the small-angle approximation ($\sin\theta \approx \theta$ in radians), the restoring torque yields simple harmonic motion:

T₀ = 2π · √(L / g)

Key physical implications:

  • Mass Independence: Period $T_0$ does not depend on the mass $m$ of the bob!
  • Length Dependence: Period is proportional to $\sqrt{L}$. To double the period, you must quadruple the string length!
  • Gravity Dependence: Period is inversely proportional to $\sqrt{g}$. On the Moon ($g = 1.62\text{ m/s}^2$), a pendulum swings much slower.

3. Exact Large-Angle Nonlinear Breakdown

When the initial release angle $\theta_0$ is large, the period increases according to the Legendre elliptic series expansion:

T = T₀ · (1 + 1/16 · θ₀² + 11/3072 · θ₀⁴ + ...)

At $\theta_0 = 30^\circ$, the actual period is only ~1.7% longer than $T_0$. But at $\theta_0 = 90^\circ$, it is over ~18% longer!
