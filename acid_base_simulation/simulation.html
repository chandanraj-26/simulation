📚 Acid-Base Indicator Theory & Formulations

1. What is an Acid-Base Indicator?

An acid-base indicator is a weak organic acid ($HIn$) or weak organic base whose conjugate acid and base forms possess distinctly different colors due to structural rearrangement of their chromophore $\pi$-electron system:

HIn (Acid Form Color) ⇌ H⁺(aq) + In⁻ (Conjugate Base Color)

2. Henderson-Hasselbalch Equation for Indicators

The equilibrium dissociation constant is given by:

K_In = [H⁺][In⁻] / [HIn] ⟹ pH = pK_In + log([In⁻] / [HIn])

The human eye perceives the acid color when $[HIn] \ge 10 \times [In^-]$ ($pH \le pK_{In} - 1$), and the base color when $[In^-] \ge 10 \times [HIn]$ ($pH \ge pK_{In} + 1$). The effective color transition interval is:

ΔpH = pK_In ± 1.0 (Span of ~2.0 pH units)
